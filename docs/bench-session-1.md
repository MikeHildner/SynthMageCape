# Bench Session 1: Station Setup, Practice, Shifter Board, First Strip

Goal by the end: one real 80 cm lapel strip lit from WLED, through the level shifter, powered from the bank. Stop at any step where a part has not arrived; every step before it is still worth doing.

## 0. What you need on the bench

Have these out before starting:

- YIHUA station, its tips, the brass tip cleaner, solder, flux, wick
- Flush cutters, wire strippers (scissors and a knife work if those have not arrived)
- Heat shrink assortment and the heat gun (or a lighter)
- The 5 m WS2812B roll
- JST-SM 3-pin pigtails (WESIRI, the 10 pairs)
- Perfboard pack, DIP-14 socket, one SN74AHCT125N, 220 ohm resistors, one 1000 uF capacitor
- 20 AWG red/black wire, 24 AWG wire
- The Dupont jumper wires that came in the INMP441 bag
- The USB-A to bare-wire cable, the USB meter, one power bank
- The ESP32-S3 board and its USB cable
- Multimeter

Not needed today: COB strip, bullet strings, WAGO connectors, fuse holder, switch, buttons, box, mic.

## 1. Station setup (10 minutes)

1. Fit the smallest chisel tip or a fine conical tip from the kit. A chisel about 1.5 to 2 mm wide is the best all-rounder for strip pads.
2. Set the temperature to **330 C / 625 F** for 63/37 solder. Hotter does not help; it burns flux and lifts pads.
3. When it reaches temperature, melt a little solder onto the tip until the end is shiny ("tinning"), then wipe it in the brass cleaner. Do this every few joints and before putting the iron down. A shiny tip transfers heat; a dull grey one does not.
4. Ventilation: a window open or a small fan blowing the fume away from your face. Wash hands after the session; no food at the bench. Keep the iron in its stand whenever it is not in your hand.

## 2. Practice on scrap (30 to 60 minutes, do not skip)

1. From the end of the roll that does **not** have the arrow pointing into it (see section 4 for how to read the arrows), cut off a 10 cm piece on a cut line. This is scrap.
2. Peel back about 1 cm of the silicone sleeve at one end to expose the three copper pads. Trim the sleeve with the flush cutters or a knife.
3. **Tin the pads:** touch a dab of flux to each pad. Put the iron tip on the pad, feed a little solder in, lift. One second per pad. You want a small shiny dome on each.
4. **Tin the wires:** strip 3 mm of a 24 AWG wire, twist, flux, touch iron and solder to it. The strands should fuse into one silver wire.
5. **Join:** lay the tinned wire on the tinned pad, press the iron on top for about one second until both melt together, hold the wire still, lift the iron, keep holding for two seconds. Done.
6. Do this on all three pads, then tug each wire. It should not move. Repeat on the other end of the scrap until the joints look like small smooth domes and take under two seconds each.

Common first-timer problems and fixes:

- Solder balls up and will not stick: not enough heat on the pad, or no flux. Flux first, hold the iron a touch longer.
- Pad goes dull and the sleeve melts around it: too long on the pad. One second, lift.
- Two pads bridged with solder: touch the wick to the bridge with the iron on top; it soaks the excess up.
- Wire falls off when tugged: it moved while cooling. Hold still for two seconds after lifting the iron.

## 3. Build the shifter and distribution board (45 minutes)

This is the small board that lives in the box: USB power in, capacitor, level shifter chip, four data lines out through resistors. Use the 5 x 7 cm perfboard.

The circuit itself is in KiCad: `hardware/shifter-board/shifter-board.kicad_pro` (open it in KiCad 10, or read `diagrams/shifter-board-schematic.pdf`). It is generated by `tools/make_shifter_schematic.py` and passes KiCad's electrical rules check. When the board drawings and the schematic disagree, the schematic wins.

The same circuit is also laid out as a printed board in `hardware/shifter-board/shifter-board.kicad_pcb` (`tools/make_shifter_pcb.py`), with a 3D view in `diagrams/shifter-board-3d.png` and the copper in `diagrams/shifter-board-layout.pdf`. It passes KiCad's design rules check. It is an option for the final build, not the plan for this session: the perfboard steps below still apply.

Board four (7 November 2026) uses a different layout from the drawings below, planned in KiCad and checked against the schematic: `hardware/perfboard-4/perfboard-4.kicad_pro`, with the step-by-step build in `perfboard-4-build.md`. Use that guide for board four; the steps below are the board-three version and stay for reference.

Reference drawings, all drawn with the board held so the numbers 01 to 18 run across the top and the letters A to X run down the sides (rebuild with `python tools/draw_shifter_board.py <0|1|2> <out.png>`):

- `diagrams/perfboard-grid.png`: the bare board and its hole names. A hole is letter then number, so P12 is row P, column 12.
- `diagrams/shifter-board-step1-rails.png`: socket, the two rails, capacitor.
- `diagrams/shifter-board-full.png`: every wire, resistor and lead.

Chip pinout for the SN74AHCT125N, with the notch at the top and pin 1 at the top left, numbers going down the left side 1 to 7 and up the right side 8 to 14:

| Pin | Function | Connect to |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 4, 10, 13 | Output enable (active low) | GND |
| 2 | Input A | ESP32 GPIO 4 |
| 3 | Output A | 220 ohm, then data line 1 (left front) |
| 5 | Input B | ESP32 GPIO 5 |
| 6 | Output B | 220 ohm, then data line 2 (right front) |
| 7 | GND | GND |
| 8 | Output C | 220 ohm, then data line 4 (right drape) |
| 9 | Input C | ESP32 GPIO 7 |
| 11 | Output D | 220 ohm, then data line 3 (left drape) |
| 12 | Input D | ESP32 GPIO 6 |
| 14 | VCC | 5 V |

Steps:

1. Solder the DIP socket to the perfboard, notch matching how you will orient the chip. Solder two diagonal pins first, check it sits flat, then the rest. Do not put the chip in yet.
2. Make a 5 V rail and a GND rail: two rows of holes along one edge, bridged with bare wire or solder. Solder the 1000 uF capacitor across them, its **long leg to 5 V**, the stripe side to GND. Getting this backwards damages the capacitor. Sequence that worked on the third board: solder the rail wire to every ring **except** G01 and G03, test, then push the capacitor legs through those two open holes from the front and solder them on the back with the rail wire. Once a rail is soldered its holes are full, so every later wire that joins a rail is laid on the rail joint on the back and melted in, never pushed through. (The front ring works too; the board is plated through.)
3. Wire pins 1, 4, 10, 13 and 7 to the GND rail. Wire pin 14 to the 5 V rail.
4. Solder one 220 ohm resistor from each of pins 3, 6, 8, 11 to a free hole, then a short 24 AWG wire from each resistor to a JST-SM **female** pigtail's middle (data) wire. Solder each pigtail's red wire to the 5 V rail and black to GND. You now have four output plugs. Today only output 1 needs a pigtail; add the rest when the WAGOs arrive if you prefer.
5. Inputs: cut two of the Dupont jumper wires in half. Solder the cut ends to pins 2 and 5 (GPIO 4 and 5 lines) and to a GND rail hole; the female plug ends go onto the ESP32's header pins. Add the other two inputs later.
6. Power in: strip the USB-A cable's bare end, red to the 5 V rail, black to GND. Put a drop of hot glue or a loop of wire through two holes as strain relief; this cable gets pulled.
7. ESP32 power: one more Dupont half from the 5 V rail to the board's **5V** pin and one from GND to a **GND** pin. The board's own regulator makes its 3.3 V from that.
8. **Before inserting the chip:** multimeter on continuity, probe 5 V rail to GND rail. It must not beep. Then plug the USB cable into the bank and read the rail with the meter on DC volts: about 5.0 V. Unplug.
9. Insert the chip, notch matching the socket. Press evenly; the legs bend easily.

## 4. Cut and terminate the first lapel strip (20 minutes)

1. Look at the strip. Printed arrows show the data direction. Data goes **in** at the end the arrows point away from. The pads at a cut line are labelled 5V, DI or DO, and GND.
2. Measure 80 cm from the roll's data-in end, which is the end you will keep the factory connector on if its arrows allow. If the factory connector is on the data-in end, you do not need to solder a pigtail at all today: plug the female pigtail from the shifter board straight into it. Count the LEDs: 48.
3. Cut on the cut line with the flush cutters, through the middle of the pads so both sides keep half a pad.
4. If you had to make a new data-in end: expose the pads, tin them, solder a JST-SM **male** pigtail: red to 5V, middle to DI, black to GND. Match the pigtail's wire order to the strip's pad order; they are not always the same, so read the pads.
5. Slide 2 cm of heat shrink over the joint and shrink it. Then one more piece 1 cm further up the wires as the strain relief anchor.

## 5. First light (15 minutes)

1. ESP32 board: GPIO 4 Dupont lead plugged onto the pin labelled 4, the GND lead onto GND, the 5V lead onto 5V. Nothing on the ESP32's USB ports; it is powered from the rail now.
2. Plug the strip into the output 1 pigtail. Plug the USB cable into the bank through the meter.
3. WLED, Config, LED & Hardware: add a second output with the **+** button. Output 2: WS281x RGB, 55 mA, GRB, length **48**, Data GPIO **4**. Leave output 1 (GPIO 48, length 1) alone. Set **Maximum PSU Current** to **1500** mA for today. Save.
4. Main page, Segments tab: you should now see the strip as a second segment. Pick a color. 48 LEDs light.
5. Checks: colors match the swatches (if red and green are swapped, change the color order to RGB). Set white at full brightness and read the meter; it should cap near 1.5 A and the limiter dims the strip to hold it. Run an effect like "Rainbow" for ten minutes; touch the strip end joint and the chip; both should be no more than warm.

## What done looks like

A strip lit from WLED through the shifter board on bank power, the meter showing the limiter holding current, and no joint that moves when tugged. Log the current readings. Next session: the other three lapel pieces, the shoulders, and the collar COB last.
