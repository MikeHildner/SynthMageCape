# Synth Mage Cape: What the Garment Needs from the Electronics

For whoever makes the cape. This sheet tells you, in sewing terms, what the lighting hardware needs from the garment. The look, fabric, and silhouette are yours to decide. This sheet only covers where the hardware goes, how big it is, and how it has to come out.

Prepared in November 2026 by Mike Hildner (electronics and firmware), after the electronics were built and tested.

## Where things stand

- **Built and working:** the control box, both lapel light pieces, and the wireless link from the keyboard. The lights follow what Mike plays: each key makes a ripple at its own height on the lapel, harder playing is brighter, the pedal holds the glow. Mike can show this on a stand-in cape before you start.
- **Built as prototypes, on the stand-in cape:** the collar light, the shoulder lights, and the string lights below the lapels. They work and follow the keyboard too, but their sizes and positions are my guesses. The final ones depend on the real garment, so they wait for your input.
- **Nothing is fixed that sewing would have to work around.** The lapel pieces can be rebuilt to a different length, and every other piece will be cut to your measurements.

## How I would like to work with you

The lights are not all finished, and I do not want you to wait for them. I would like to do this in stages, with a gap in the middle. Please tell me at the start whether that suits you, since it affects your schedule and your price.

1. **Before any fabric is cut:** we talk through this sheet and I show you the working lapel. A few things have to be decided at this point because they are hard to add later: the width of the lapel facing, a collar stiff enough to carry a strip, a lining that cables can run behind, and shoulder pieces that pass the reach test.
2. **You make the cape, deliberately unfinished where the lights go.** The lining stays open, the channels are not sewn yet, and the hem is left for last.
3. **I pin on the lights and the control box,** try the cape at the keyboard, and adjust until it is right. This is the gap: it may take me a few weeks.
4. **You finish it around the real hardware:** channels, mesh, the pouch, openings for the cables, and closing everything up.

What can wait until stage 4: the exact length of each light piece, how the string lights below the lapels are held in place (section 4), the mesh, and the pouch.

If you would work differently, tell me. You know how a garment goes together and I do not.

## The short version

- Lit zones: the collar outline, two lapels, two shoulders, and strings of small dot lights below the lapels and along an outer line on each side. Nothing lit within 15 cm of the hem.
- Each front edge gets one mesh-faced channel, about 30 mm wide, for the lapel lights. Whether it continues below the lapel for the dot lights is your call (section 4).
- Everything electrical is removable. Nothing is sewn in permanently. Cleaning the cape means pulling all of it out first.
- Plug-in points: one at the bottom of each lapel, near the waist, and two at the top of the front edge on the pouch side, one for the collar and one for the shoulders. There is a drawing of the cable routes further down.
- One pouch on a belt at the hip holds the battery and the control box.
- The strip lights go behind a black mesh, so the cape looks like a plain black cape when they are off.
- Nothing connects Mike to the keyboard. The link is wireless, so the cape has no trailing cable.
- Before any channels are sewn, we do a plain-fabric mockup and a reach test at the keyboard with foam shoulder pieces.

## Zone by zone

Lengths are planning figures. You set the final ones at the fitting. I will cut the hardware to match.

### 1. Collar: a glowing line outlining the whole collar

A prototype is working on the stand-in cape. The final one is cut to your collar.

- The light is a thin flexible strip that gives an unbroken line of light with no visible dots. It bends along its length but not sideways. It runs up each of the two straight front edges of the high collar, about 30 cm per side, then continues along the full top rim of the collar around the back of the neck, about 50 cm. From the front the collar reads as one outlined shape, as in the render.
- Laid flat against the collar face, the strip follows the rim's curve around the neck without trouble. The corner where the front edge turns into the top rim is a cut and a hidden join on my side; it just needs a few millimetres of room in the channel at that corner.
- It needs a stiff collar to sit on. Stiff interfacing or thin foam in the collar is ideal.
- Channel: allow about 16 mm wide along the front edges and the top rim, with a black mesh face. The strip sits in a clear tube about 9 mm across, and the channel has to wrap around it; the collar dummy (see the end of this sheet) will give the exact size. The strip is a pale stripe when it is off, so the mesh matters more here than anywhere else.
- The collar light is one piece, all the way round. It has one plug, on a short cable at the bottom of one front edge, on the same side as the pouch.

### 2. Lapels: the main event, two strips side by side per lapel

Built and working.

- Each lapel is one piece: two flexible strips side by side, each about 12 mm wide and 80 cm long, lying 5 to 15 mm apart. Each strip carries 48 small lights.
- The two strips are joined at the top (the collar end) by a small loop of three thin wires. The loop needs about 3 cm of room above the top of the strips.
- There are two short cables with connectors, both at the bottom end. One takes the cable from the pouch. That side's string of dot lights (section 4) plugs into the other. A lapel has no other connections.
- If connections at the top would suit the garment better, tell me. It can be done, but the string of dot lights starts at the bottom of the lapel, so it would then need a cable run down to it.
- **The length can change, but only by rebuilding the piece.** I can build any length in steps of about 17 mm (one light). Please give me the final lapel length before I build the final pair.
- Channel: 28 to 32 mm wide, with a black mesh face, running down each front edge from the collar. Leaving 10 to 15 mm of air between the strips and the mesh softens the individual dots into a bar, so a slightly padded or stood-off channel looks better than mesh lying directly on the strips.
- **Question for the first fitting: how wide will the lapel facing be?** Two strips want at least 50 mm of lapel. If the lapel comes in under 35 mm, I will drop to one strip per lapel and the channel shrinks to about 14 mm.

### 3. Shoulders: two short strips side by side on soft epaulets

A prototype pair is working on the stand-in cape. Mike would like to have these if the reach test allows.

- Each shoulder is one piece, built like a short lapel: two flexible strips side by side, each about 12 mm wide and 20 cm long, lying 5 to 15 mm apart. Each strip carries 12 small lights. It reads as a double bar on the shoulder, as in the render.
- The strips run from the neck out to the shoulder edge. At the shoulder edge they are joined by a small loop of three thin wires, which needs about 3 cm of room beyond the ends of the strips. The connections are at the neck end.
- The shoulder on the pouch side takes the cable from the pouch. The other shoulder is fed from it by a thin three-wire link, about 30 cm long, that runs behind the neck at the base of the collar. The link has a connector in the middle, so each shoulder piece still comes out on its own. It wants a short hidden channel or a few thread loops to hold it.
- The length can change in steps of about 17 mm (one light), by rebuilding the piece.
- The shoulder pieces must be soft or semi-rigid: EVA foam or stiff interfacing shaped at the fitting. Not hard printed shells. A rigid piece on the shoulder joint is the thing most likely to stop Mike reaching across the keyboard, which is the test the whole costume has to pass.
- The strips bend over the shoulder curve fine, as long as the curve is in one direction.
- Channel: 28 to 32 mm wide, with a black mesh face, as on the lapels. So each shoulder piece needs a face about 5 cm wide and 25 cm long.
- If the reach test says the epaulets have to go, nothing else is affected.

### 4. Below the lapels: strings of small dot lights

A prototype is working on the stand-in cape, one string per side.

The render shows two more lines of light dots on each side: one continuing down the front edge below the lapel, and an outer line that starts near the waist and angles out towards the hem. (I call it the outer line because I do not know whether your pattern has a seam there; the line in the render is what I mean.)

- **What they are:** a string of tiny lights, each under a small bead of clear resin, about 10 cm apart on three thin wires. A string of 50 is 5 m long and very light. I will weigh one for you.
- **One string per side.** It plugs in at the bottom of the lapel, runs down the front edge, crosses over to the outer line a little above the hem, and climbs the outer line. The lights in the crossing stay switched off, so it does not show. The drawing in the next section shows the path. On the stand-in cape that path uses 17 of the 50 lights: 6 down the front edge, 4 in the crossing, 7 up the outer line.
- **A string can go where the fabric folds and swings.** That is why I chose it for this part of the cape; the strips on the lapels cannot.
- **The spacing is fixed at 10 cm** by the wire between the lights. A string cannot be stretched. It can be made to look wider-spaced, because which lights are on is software.
- **Leftover string** is coiled and tucked away inside the lining, or I cut it off once the path is settled.
- **The hem rule: nothing lit within 15 cm of the hem.** Anything at floor level gets stepped on and kicked.

The questions for you:

- **How would you hold a string in place?** A narrow sleeve, thread loops around the wire between the lights, or something better that you know. It has to come out for cleaning, and nothing may be stitched through the wires.
- **Where should the outer line run on your pattern?**
- **Mesh or no mesh over the dots?** They are small points of light, not a pale stripe like the strips, so they may not need hiding.

**One thing you should know.** The strings are very thin wire, and I have not yet tested how long they survive repeated folding. If they turn out too fragile, the fallback is no lights below the lapels. The lapels carry the show; the lower lights are an extra, not a requirement. Nothing you sew in stage 2 depends on them.

## The plug-in points, and where the cables run

[[wiring-drawing]]

The pouch with the control box and battery is on a belt, worn on the body under the cape. Four cables leave it. The drawing shows the pouch on Mike's right hip; which side is not decided. The side matters, because the collar and shoulder plugs go on the same side as the pouch. Please tell me which hip suits the garment before I build the final pieces.

| Where | What plugs in | Cable arriving from |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom of each lapel, near the waist | The lapel. That side's string of dot lights plugs into the lapel at the same place. | The pouch. A short run on the pouch side; around Mike's back along the belt for the other side. |
| Top of the front edge on the pouch side, where it meets the collar | The collar light | The pouch, then up inside the lining of that front edge. |
| The shoulder on the pouch side, at its neck end | That shoulder piece. The other shoulder is fed from it by the link behind the neck. | The pouch, up the same front edge, beside the collar cable. |

- Getting dressed goes: belt, then cape, then four plugs.
- The cape is open at the front, so no cable can cross there. The far lapel is reached behind Mike, along the belt. The far shoulder is reached by the link behind the neck.
- All four connections are built and working on the stand-in cape. The route of the two cables that go up to the collar and the shoulder is still yours to choose.
- **An alternative route for those two cables:** together up the centre of the back, inside the lining, then round under the collar to the front on the pouch side. It is one hidden channel either way. Tell me which suits your seams.

Each plug is a small locking connector on three thin wires. I will give you a real pair so you can size openings against it. A cable from the pouch up to the collar or the shoulder is about 1 m of thin three-wire cable and wants a hidden channel in the lining; the two can share one channel. Each cable also needs a small loop or tab near the connector that it can be tied to, so a tug on the cape pulls on the fabric and never on the plug.

Rules of thumb for everything that plugs or is sewn in:

- Leave a finger-width of slack at every connector.
- Anchor each light strip 2 to 3 cm in from its ends, never right at the end. The ends are the weak points.
- No light strip goes anywhere the fabric creases sharply.
- Nothing hard or wired should be able to touch Mike's skin or snag the keyboard.
- Never pin or stitch through a strip or a wire. Stitch around them.

## The pouch at the hip

- Contents: a power bank and a control box. The control box is about 97 x 91 x 36 mm with rounded corners, and its cables all leave from one of the 91 mm ends. The power bank is the size of a large phone, somewhere between 100 x 60 x 25 mm and 150 x 70 x 30 mm. I will bring both so the pouch is sized against the real objects, and I will weigh them beforehand. Worn on a belt at the hip, under the cape.
- **The control box may get slightly smaller or change shape once.** The present box is a first version. I will tell you before the pouch is cut if it changes.
- **Microphone: probably not needed.** The control box can carry a small microphone that listens to the band. It would need the pouch's outer face to stay uncovered, with a mesh window. Now that the lights follow the keyboard directly, Mike doubts the microphone is worth having. Please tell me whether an exposed pouch face is easy or awkward for you. If it is awkward, the microphone goes and the pouch can be completely hidden.
- **No metal around the pouch.** The control box talks to the keyboard by radio (Bluetooth). Metallic fabric, foil lining, or a metal plate over the pouch would block it. Ordinary fabric, foam, and plastic are fine.
- Three small buttons and a power switch sit on the pouch flap, on wires about 40 cm long. Mike must be able to reach them without help while wearing the cape. The power switch is the real off switch for everything.
- Four cables leave the pouch: two to the lapel bottoms, and two up the front edge on the pouch side, to the collar and to the shoulder. Only the cable for the far lapel goes around Mike's back along the belt.

## Cleaning and access

- Every strip, string, cable, and the pouch comes out before cleaning. None of it can be washed, steamed, or dry-cleaned.
- Channels therefore need an opening at one end with concealed snaps or hook-and-loop, wide enough to slide the strip out. For the lapels, the opening must pass the pair of strips together with their joining loop, or the two connectors; tell me which end you would rather open.
- The hidden cable channel in the lining needs an opening at each end. So does the short one for the link behind the neck.
- Connectors are marked so Mike can reassemble it without help.
- In bench testing nothing ran warm to the touch, so the lights do not need ventilation.

## Mesh over everything

A black diffusion mesh over every strip, so with the lights off the cape reads as a plain black cape with the silhouette doing the work. Where you can, a little stand-off between the lights and the mesh makes the dots blur into a glow.

**Please do not buy mesh in quantity before we have tested a scrap.** The lights run deliberately dim: this is a costume for a dark stage, and the aim is a glow that does not overwhelm the other players or the audience. At that brightness I do not yet know how much a black mesh takes away, and a dense mesh could swallow the light. The test is simple: hold the scrap over the working lapel in a dark room.

## The first fitting

This belongs to stage 1, before the real fabric is cut:

1. Plain fabric mockup, no lights.
2. Foam shoulder mockups on. Mike reaches across the full width of the keyboard (88 keys) and to the controls at both ends. No cape panel touches the keys. This decides whether the shoulders get lights.
3. Decide the lapel facing width (50 mm or more preferred) and the lapel length.
4. Confirm the collar front edges are straight and stiff enough to carry a strip.
5. Confirm safe hem clearance from the stage floor.
6. Look at where the string lights below the lapels will run (section 4), the outer line in particular, so the pattern leaves room for them.
7. Choose which hip the pouch sits on, and the route for the two cables to the collar and the shoulder: up the front edge on the pouch side, or up the centre back.

These can wait until the lights are pinned on (stage 3 or 4):

- How the string lights below the lapels are held in place.
- Where the pouch sits on the belt, and whether its outer face can show.
- Mesh samples held over the working lights in a darkened room.
